How to check a monitor for pixel problems

Clean the display gently before testing and view it from a normal position. Open each colour in turn and scan the panel in a consistent pattern. A pixel that remains dark or shows an unusual colour across several backgrounds may be defective, but dust, reflections, image scaling, and viewing angle can be misleading.

What the colour screens help you see

  • White and light colours: useful for spotting dark specks and uneven brightness.
  • Black: can help reveal bright spots or uneven backlight glow on LCD displays.
  • Red, green, and blue: help identify pixels or subpixels that do not change as expected.
  • Gray: useful for noticing some forms of brightness non-uniformity.
  • Alignment grid: provides a simple visual reference for screen geometry; it is not a precision calibration tool.

What are dead and stuck pixels?

A dead pixel may remain dark, while a stuck pixel or subpixel may remain bright or show one colour. The appearance varies by display technology and defect. One short browser test cannot conclusively diagnose every panel issue.

Checking backlight uniformity

For an LCD, display a black screen in a dim room and look for unusually bright areas near edges or corners. Some glow can be normal and can vary with viewing angle and brightness. OLED displays behave differently and may not show LCD-style backlight bleed.

Frequently asked questions

Can this tool repair a dead pixel?

No. It displays test patterns only. It cannot repair physical panel damage or guarantee that a stuck pixel can be restored.

Does the test work on mobile?

The colour and grid patterns are designed to adapt to mobile screens. Fullscreen behaviour depends on the browser and operating system, so an on-screen exit control is provided as a fallback.

Does it upload a screenshot or scan my display?

No screenshot or display scan is uploaded by this page. The tool changes the page’s displayed pattern; it cannot directly inspect the physical panel or detect a defect automatically.
